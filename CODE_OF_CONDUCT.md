# Code of conduct

GitStudio follows the [Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/).
It applies in this repository and everywhere else the project is represented:
issues, pull requests, discussions, and the Merge Studio repository.

In short: we want taking part in GitStudio to be free of harassment for
everyone, whatever their age, body, disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, appearance, race, caste,
colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

- **Do:** be kind and patient, respect differing opinions and experience, give
  and gracefully accept constructive feedback, and own your mistakes.
- **Don't:** use sexualized language or imagery; troll, insult or make
  personal or political attacks; harass anyone, in public or in private;
  publish someone else's private information without their permission; or do
  anything else that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour worries you, tell the maintainers privately: open this
repository's [**Security** tab](https://github.com/GitStudioHQ/gitstudio/security)
and click **Report a vulnerability**. The report is visible only to you and
the maintainers, and it is the right place for conduct reports too, not only
security ones. We will review every report promptly and fairly, and respect
the privacy of whoever reports.

## Enforcement

The maintainers decide what a breach of this code calls for, following the
Covenant's
[enforcement guidelines](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/#enforcement-guidelines):
a private correction, a warning, a temporary ban, or a permanent ban, as the
behaviour warrants.
